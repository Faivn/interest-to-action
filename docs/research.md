# Research & Product Discovery

## 1. Research Context

The idea for Interest to Action came from my own experience of frequently discovering new interests, courses, skills, and project ideas. I often capture these intentions in lists, but many remain unfinished.

I wanted to understand why collecting intentions does not always translate into action, what existing productivity tools already solve, and whether AI could help bridge this gap.

I used NotebookLM to synthesise a collection of research sources concerning decision-making, information overload, unfinished intentions, self-regulated learning, and AI-assisted planning.

## 2. Research Questions

The research explored five questions:

1. Why do people struggle to translate intentions into action?
2. How can collecting too many interests contribute to decision-making difficulties?
3. What do existing productivity and knowledge-management tools already do well?
4. Where might existing approaches leave users with additional effort or unresolved decisions?
5. How could AI support action planning without taking control away from the user?

## 3. Key Findings

### 3.1 Capturing an Intention Is Not the Same as Acting on It

The NotebookLM synthesis highlighted research on the intention–behaviour gap: people can genuinely intend to achieve something without following through.

It also discussed the distinction between deciding on a goal and implementing it. Choosing what to do is a different challenge from deciding when, where, and how to begin.

**Product implication:** The product should focus on helping users move from an existing intention to a concrete next action, rather than simply providing another place to store ideas.

### 3.2 Organisation Can Become Work in Itself

The synthesis identified potential problems with elaborate productivity systems, including maintaining databases, organising notes, updating statuses, and managing tracking structures.

These activities can compete with the actual work users want to accomplish.

**Product implication:** The experience should minimise setup, manual categorisation, and ongoing maintenance.

### 3.3 Existing Tools Already Solve Important Parts of the Problem

Note-taking applications, task managers, bookmarking tools, and habit trackers offer useful ways to capture, organise, retrieve, and track information.

The opportunity is not to assume these tools are ineffective. It is to investigate whether some users need more support in turning an accumulated intention into a realistic action.

**Product implication:** Interest to Action should complement the value of idea capture while exploring a more guided transition from intention to action.

### 3.4 AI Can Support Planning Without Replacing User Judgment

The research synthesis discussed AI as a potential co-regulator: helping users evaluate options, reflect on their choices, and plan actions while preserving their agency.

It also highlighted risks associated with over-automation, excessive guidance, and AI-generated suggestions that do not reflect the user's actual goals.

**Product implication:** AI should offer a small number of relevant suggestions that users can edit, accept, or reject.

## 4. Research Limitations

The findings above are based on the sources and synthesis available in NotebookLM. The synthesis does not, by itself, establish how widespread this specific problem is or demonstrate that the proposed product would improve follow-through.

The following questions remain open:

- Which users experience this problem most acutely?
- What do they currently do when an interest remains unfinished?
- Do existing tools already solve the problem adequately for some users?
- Would users find AI-assisted action planning more useful than creating their own next steps?
- Would the experience remain useful beyond an initial interaction?

These questions require further source verification and, where possible, feedback from prospective users.

## 5. Product Opportunity

The opportunity is to explore a lightweight experience that helps users convert one accumulated interest into a concrete, user-approved action plan without requiring a complex productivity system.

The proposed initial workflow is:

1. Capture an interest.
2. Clarify the user's intended outcome or main obstacle.
3. Generate a small number of practical action plans with AI.
4. Let the user select or edit a plan.
5. Save the selected plan for later follow-through.

## 6. Product Decision

I chose to focus the initial prototype on the transition from intention to action.

Rather than building a comprehensive task manager, habit tracker, or personal knowledge-management system, the prototype will test one specific interaction: whether AI can help a user turn an unfinished interest into a manageable next step.

This is a product hypothesis to explore, not a claim of validated market demand.
